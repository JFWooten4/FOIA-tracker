Why bother?

A good FOIA request turns “I wonder” into a traceable search for agency records.

It is especially useful when the thing you are looking for is older, hard to discover, not posted online, or likely to live in internal agency files rather than a public database.

01

Reach beyond the website

Ask for existing SEC records that are not already easy to find in EDGAR, rulemaking pages, enforcement releases, or other public collections.

02

Get a trackable request

A submitted request receives an agency tracking number, creating a concrete record you can follow through acknowledgment, processing, and response.

03

Create reusable research

When releasable records come back, posting them publicly can save the next researcher from having to rediscover the same material alone.

04

Learn from the process

Scope questions, fee decisions, searches, exemptions, and productions all teach future requesters how to frame better requests.

Start your own

Make the SEC search for records, not for your answer.

FOIA is a records-access law. The strongest requests identify existing records with enough detail that agency staff can reasonably locate them.

  1. 1

    Check what is already public

    Search SEC.gov, EDGAR, rulemaking materials, enforcement pages, and the SEC’s frequently requested records first.

  2. 2

    Describe the record set

    Use a subject, date range, likely office or custodian, document type, names, and distinctive terms when you know them.

  3. 3

    Keep each request focused

    One subject per request is easier to search, easier to track, and consistent with the SEC’s own guidance for potentially faster processing.

  4. 4

    Publish what comes back

    Add the correspondence and released records to a public tracker so others can follow the request and reuse the result.

What FOIA does not do: it generally does not require the SEC to conduct research for you, analyze data, answer questions, or create a new record. Records may also be withheld in whole or part under applicable FOIA exemptions.
