# SEC FOIA request — Department of Justice comments filed May 17, 1978

Please provide an electronic copy of the complete Comments of the United States Department of Justice filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission on May 17, 1978 concerning National Securities Clearing Corporation and the national clearance and settlement system, including all attachments, exhibits, appendices, transmittal correspondence, supplemental submissions, revisions, and any cover or index material identifying the filing.

Please also provide any public-file correspondence necessary to identify the SEC file in which the comments were placed. If the submission is maintained only on microfiche, microfilm, retired paper, Federal Records Center/offsite storage, or legacy Public Reference Room holdings, please search those holdings and provide the best available reproduction.

## Sources identifying the records

The D.C. Circuit's decision in Bradford National Clearing Corp. v. SEC expressly cites "Comments of the United States Department of Justice (filed with the SEC, May 17, 1978)." The opinion describes DOJ as having opposed NSCC registration before the Commission and cites pages 27–29 of the DOJ comments when discussing objections to geographic price mutualization, including concerns that NSCC could price regional services below cost and underprice competing regional clearing agencies. See [Bradford National Clearing Corp. v. SEC, 590 F.2d 1085 (D.C. Cir. 1978)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/590/1085/224738/).

The Commission's January 1978 notice and later hearing notices identify File No. 600-15 as the file for the national clearance and settlement hearings and invite written submissions concerning issues including pricing, competition, and inter-clearing-agency interfaces. See [SEC News Digest, Jan. 25, 1978](https://www.sec.gov/news/digest/1978/dig012578.pdf) and [43 FR 16269 (Apr. 17, 1978)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1978-04-17/pdf/FR-1978-04-17.pdf).

## Fee category / waiver

Consistent with the SEC's recent classification of WhyDRS as a Media requester, these records are sought for source-linked public research and editorial analysis and will be disseminated freely to the public, not for a commercial use. I request treatment as a representative of the news media under 17 CFR § 200.80(g)(2)(vi) and a waiver or reduction of fees under 5 USC § 552(a)(4)(A)(iii) and 17 CFR § 200.80(g)(12), because the submission is a substantive federal antitrust-policy comment on competition and pricing in the national clearance and settlement system.
