# SEC FOIA request — DOJ letters dated July 23 and November 19, 1976

Please provide electronic copies of the following two United States Department of Justice letters submitted in connection with National Securities Clearing Corporation's clearing-agency registration proceeding in SEC File No. 600-15:

- the Department of Justice letter dated July 23, 1976; and
- the Department of Justice letter dated November 19, 1976.

Please include any attachments, exhibits, appendices, transmittal correspondence, or cover sheets belonging to those two letters. This request is limited to these specifically identified submissions rather than the complete File No. 600-15 record. If the letters are maintained only on microfiche, microfilm, retired paper, Federal Records Center/offsite storage, or legacy Public Reference Room holdings, please search those holdings and provide the best available reproduction.

## Sources identifying the records

The Commission's January 13, 1977 order granting NSCC registration expressly cites "letters of United States Department of Justice, July 23 and November 19, 1976" in its discussion of the effects of NSCC's registration on competition among clearing agencies. See [Securities Exchange Act Release No. 34-13163, 42 FR 3916, 3931 (Jan. 21, 1977)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1977-01-21/pdf/FR-1977-01-21.pdf).

The Commission also stated that the NSCC registration materials, related correspondence, memoranda of meetings, and hearing transcripts were maintained in public File No. 600-15. See [41 FR 49721, 49723 (Nov. 10, 1976)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1976-11-10/pdf/FR-1976-11-10.pdf).

## Fee category / waiver

Consistent with the SEC's recent classification of WhyDRS as a Media requester, these records are sought for source-linked public research and editorial analysis and will be disseminated freely to the public, not for a commercial use. I request treatment as a representative of the news media under 17 CFR § 200.80(g)(2)(vi) and a waiver or reduction of fees under 5 USC § 552(a)(4)(A)(iii) and 17 CFR § 200.80(g)(12), because the letters concern federal antitrust-policy views on competition among clearing agencies during the Commission's establishment of the national clearance and settlement system.
